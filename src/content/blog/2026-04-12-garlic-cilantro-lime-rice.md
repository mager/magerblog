This is one of those side dishes that does more work than it should. It's still just rice, but garlic gives it depth, cilantro makes it feel alive, and lime pulls the whole thing into focus.

The key move is adding the lime **after** the rice is cooked. Not in the water. If you add it to the pot up front, the acid can mess slightly with the texture and the fresh lime flavor gets dulled anyway. Finish the rice once it's fluffed and hot, and it stays brighter.

## The Method

Cook the rice mostly the normal way. Use water, salt, and garlic. You can sauté the garlic in a little butter or olive oil first if you want a softer, rounder flavor, or just add it straight to the pot for something cleaner.

While the rice cooks, pick through the cilantro and rinse it well. Dry it, then chop a lot more than you think you need. This is not the kind of dish where cilantro should feel like garnish. It should be part of the identity.

When the rice is done, fluff it first. Then add fresh lime juice, a little zest if you want more aroma, your chopped cilantro, and a final pinch of salt. Taste it. It almost always wants more lime and more salt than you expect.

## What Matters

- Pick and rinse the cilantro before anything else so it is ready when the rice is hot
- Add lime juice after cooking, not to the rice water
- Use lots of cilantro, not a polite amount
- Fluff before mixing so the rice stays light
- Finish aggressively enough that it actually tastes bright

## Why It Works

Rice can handle bold finishing ingredients. Garlic gives you the base note. Cilantro brings freshness. Lime adds acid and lift. Together, they turn a basic pot into something that tastes finished, not filler.

It works next to grilled meat, black beans, roasted vegetables, tacos, bowls, or basically anything smoky or spicy. Which is probably why I keep making it.
