Most potato salad recipes tell you to let the potatoes cool before dressing them. That's the wrong call. Hot potatoes are porous — they pull the vinaigrette in rather than just wearing it on the surface. The difference shows in the first bite.

This version skips mayo entirely. The dressing is Dijon, white wine vinegar, olive oil, and grated garlic. The herbs — chives and flat-leaf parsley — go in last so they stay bright.

## Ingredients

Serves 4–6.

- 2 lbs Yukon Gold or red potatoes, cut into 1-inch chunks
- ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 2 tbsp white wine vinegar (or fresh lemon juice)
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- ⅓ cup fresh chives, finely chopped
- ¼ cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
- Salt and black pepper to taste

## Method

**1. Cook the potatoes.**
Cover with cold salted water. Bring to a boil, reduce to a simmer, and cook until fork-tender, about 15 minutes. Drain well.

**2. Make the dressing.**
While the potatoes cook, whisk together the olive oil, Dijon, vinegar, garlic, salt, and pepper until emulsified. It should look cohesive, not separated.

**3. Dress hot.**
Pour the dressing over the potatoes immediately after draining and toss gently. The heat does the work — the starch opens up and the vinaigrette goes in rather than pooling at the bottom of the bowl.

**4. Rest, then finish with herbs.**
Let the dressed potatoes sit for 5–10 minutes. Then fold in the chives and parsley. Adding them earlier mutes their color and sharpness.

Serve warm or at room temperature. Flavor improves after an hour as everything continues to meld. Good the next day straight from the fridge too.

## Notes

- Yukon Golds hold their shape better than russets and have a natural butteriness that plays well against the acidity. Red potatoes work if that's what you have.
- Lemon juice in place of vinegar gives a slightly brighter, less winey result — both work.
- If the salad looks dry after resting, drizzle a little more olive oil before serving.
