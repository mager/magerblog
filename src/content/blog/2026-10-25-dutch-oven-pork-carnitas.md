I'm making these carnitas with a four-pound, bone-in pork shoulder and ground chile from New Mexico. I season it the night before and leave it in the fridge for a dry brine. The shoulder stays whole for a long, covered braise. Fresh orange juice, the spent orange halves, and a small cinnamon stick season the cooking juices. A short broil gives the shredded pork crisp edges while the larger pieces stay moist.

**Yield:** 6–8 servings with tortillas and sides. The four-pound starting weight includes the bone.

**Time:** Allow **8–24 hours for the overnight dry brine**, plus about 7 hours of preparation and cooking: 20 minutes to prepare, about 20 minutes to brown the pork and cook the aromatics, about 6 hours to braise, and 20 minutes to rest, shred, reduce the juices, and broil. Active work is about 45 minutes across both days. The recipe's prep and cook times exclude the overnight wait. A thick shoulder can take longer; use tenderness to decide when it is done.

**Equipment:** A 6–7-quart Dutch oven with a tight-fitting lid, a broiler-safe rimmed baking sheet, a rack and rimmed tray for the dry brine, tongs, a slotted spoon, two forks, and a measuring jug. An instant-read thermometer is useful.

## Ingredients

### Pork and spice rub

- 1 bone-in pork shoulder or Boston butt, about 4 pounds, skin removed if present; keep it whole
- 14 grams kosher salt, about 5 teaspoons Diamond Crystal or 3 teaspoons Morton, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 2 teaspoons dried Mexican oregano
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 2 teaspoons ground New Mexico red chile; use 1 teaspoon if yours is hot
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil or lard

### Aromatics and braising liquid

- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 6 garlic cloves, peeled and smashed
- 2 fresh navel oranges, scrubbed, halved, and squeezed; reserve all the juice and all 4 spent halves
- Juice of 1 lime, about 2 tablespoons
- ½ cup low-sodium chicken broth
- 2 dried bay leaves
- 1 small cinnamon stick, about 2 inches long
- Water, as needed, added in ¼-cup increments

### To serve

- Warm corn tortillas
- Diced white onion
- Chopped cilantro
- Lime wedges
- Salsa verde or your preferred salsa

## Instructions

1. **Dry-brine the pork the night before.** Trim only thick, hard fat from the **4-pound bone-in shoulder**, leaving a thin fat layer. Pat the pork dry. Mix **14 grams kosher salt (about 5 teaspoons Diamond Crystal or 3 teaspoons Morton), 1 teaspoon black pepper, 2 teaspoons Mexican oregano, 2 teaspoons cumin, 1 teaspoon garlic powder, 1 teaspoon smoked paprika, and 2 teaspoons ground New Mexico chile (or 1 teaspoon if hot)**. Rub the full mixture over the pork, including any natural creases. Leave the bone in; do not cut the shoulder into small cubes. Set the pork on a rack over a rimmed tray and refrigerate **uncovered for 8–24 hours**, on the bottom shelf where it cannot touch or drip onto other food. Without a rack, use a shallow dish that will catch the juices. The salt seasons the meat while the uncovered surface dries for browning. Keep the **2 tablespoons oil or lard**, citrus, and other braising ingredients for the next day; this step needs no liquid.

2. **Heat the oven and brown the shoulder.** The next day, set a rack in the lower third of the oven and heat to **275°F (135°C)**. Keep the pork refrigerated until you are ready to sear it. Do not rinse it or apply another batch of salt or rub. If the surface is wet, gently blot it with a paper towel without wiping off the seasoning. Heat **2 tablespoons neutral oil or lard** in the Dutch oven over medium heat. Brown the pork on its broad sides, about 3–4 minutes per side, then brown the smaller sides where practical, about 12–15 minutes total. Use tongs to turn it carefully. Reduce the heat if the spices start to scorch. Transfer the pork to a clean plate. Discard any raw juices from the dry-brining tray and wash the tray before reuse.

3. **Cook the aromatics and loosen the browned bits.** Add **1 diced medium yellow onion** to the pot and cook for 4–5 minutes, stirring. Add **6 smashed garlic cloves** and cook for 30 seconds. Pour in **½ cup low-sodium chicken broth** and scrape the bottom with a wooden spoon to release the browned bits. If any bits are black and burnt, remove them before adding the onion.

4. **Build the braise.** Add **all the juice from 2 navel oranges, all 4 spent orange halves, the juice of 1 lime (about 2 tablespoons), 2 bay leaves, and 1 small cinnamon stick**. Return the pork and any juices from its plate to the pot, with the fat side up. Place the orange halves around the pork. The liquid should cover the bottom and reach roughly one-quarter of the way up the meat. If the pot is wide or the oranges are small, add **water in ¼-cup increments** to reach that level. Do not submerge the shoulder. Bring the liquid to a gentle simmer, then cover tightly.

5. **Braise for about 6 hours.** Transfer the covered pot to the **275°F (135°C)** oven. At about 3 hours, briefly open the lid and remove the **4 orange halves and 1 cinnamon stick** with tongs or a slotted spoon. This limits bitterness from the white pith and keeps the cinnamon mild during the long cook. Check that liquid still covers the bottom; if needed, add **water in ¼-cup increments**. Cover again. Start checking tenderness at 5 hours. The pork is ready when a fork turns easily in the thickest part and the bone pulls free with little resistance. A thermometer will often read around **195–205°F (90–96°C)** away from the bone, but tenderness is the deciding test. If the meat still resists at 6 hours, continue covered in 30-minute intervals.

6. **Rest and shred.** Lift the pork onto the rimmed baking sheet, supporting it with a slotted spoon because it may fall apart. Rest for 10 minutes. Remove and discard the **2 bay leaves** from the pot, along with any orange peel or cinnamon fragments. Pull out the bone and discard any firm fat or connective tissue. Use two forks to shred the pork into coarse pieces. Leave some larger pieces for a moist center and crisp edges.

7. **Reduce and measure the juices.** While the pork rests, skim excess fat from the cooking liquid, leaving a little for flavor. Simmer the juices uncovered over medium-high heat for about 8–12 minutes, until concentrated but still pourable. Taste as they reduce; stop if they are already well seasoned. Measure **1 cup of the reduced juices** and toss it with the shredded pork. If there is less than 1 cup, add **water in ¼-cup increments**, stopping when you have 1 cup. Adjust with **kosher salt to taste** only after reducing. Reserve any extra juices for serving.

8. **Broil until crisp.** Set the broiler to high. Spread the pork in an even, shallow layer on the broiler-safe baking sheet; use two batches if it is crowded. Place the sheet about 6 inches below the element and broil for **3–5 minutes**, watching closely, until the exposed edges brown and crisp. The orange juice can burn quickly. Keep some soft pieces beneath the crisp surface. Serve at once, with extra juices on the side.

## Serving Suggestions

Fill warm corn tortillas with the pork, then add diced white onion, cilantro, salsa, and a squeeze of lime. Keep extra cooking juices on the table so each person can add a little without softening all the crisp edges.

For bowls, serve the carnitas over rice with black beans, shredded cabbage, and avocado. Broil only the pork you plan to serve immediately; keep the rest with its juices for a later meal.
