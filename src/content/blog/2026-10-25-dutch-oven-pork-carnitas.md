My earlier [Dutch Oven Pulled Pork](/blog/2025-04-27-pulled-pork/) uses a barbecue rub, vinegar, and sauce. For these carnitas, I want moist pieces of pork with deeply browned edges, ready for a warm tortilla, onion, cilantro, and salsa.

This version starts with a four-pound, bone-in shoulder and an overnight dry brine. A little orange and garlic season the pork as it cooks. I keep the rendered fat, pull the meat into large pieces, and brown those pieces just before serving. The New Mexico chile is an optional addition; the pork should still be the main flavor.

**Yield:** 6–8 servings with tortillas and sides. The four-pound starting weight includes the bone.

**Time:** Allow **8–24 hours for the overnight dry brine**, plus about 7 hours of preparation and cooking: 20 minutes to prepare, about 6 hours to braise, 15 minutes to rest and prepare the juices, and about 25 minutes to crisp in batches. Active work is about 45–60 minutes across both days. The prep and cook times above exclude the overnight wait. Start checking tenderness before six hours; a thick shoulder can take longer.

**Equipment:** A snug 5–6-quart Dutch oven with a tight-fitting lid, a rack and rimmed tray for the dry brine, a large cast-iron or heavy stainless-steel skillet, tongs, a slotted spoon, two forks, and a measuring jug. An instant-read thermometer is useful. If your Dutch oven is larger, check the liquid carefully during cooking.

## Ingredients

### Pork and overnight seasoning

- 1 bone-in pork shoulder or Boston butt, about 4 pounds, skin removed if present; keep it whole
- 14 grams kosher salt, about 5 teaspoons Diamond Crystal or 3 teaspoons Morton, plus more to taste after cooking
- 1 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 1 teaspoon dried Mexican oregano
- 1 teaspoon ground New Mexico red chile, optional; use ½ teaspoon if yours is hot

### For the Dutch oven

- ¼ cup lard
- 1 medium yellow onion, quartered
- 6 garlic cloves, peeled and smashed
- ½ cup fresh navel orange juice, from about 1–2 oranges
- 2 strips orange zest, each about 2 inches long, cut from a scrubbed orange with a vegetable peeler; avoid the white pith
- ¼ cup water, plus more as needed in ¼-cup increments
- 2 dried bay leaves
- 1 small piece of cinnamon stick, about 1 inch long, optional

### For the crisp finish

- ¼ cup fat reserved from the cooked pork, divided into 4 tablespoons; use extra lard if needed to make ¼ cup
- ¼ cup defatted cooking juices, divided into 4 tablespoons

### To serve

- Warm corn tortillas
- Diced white onion
- Chopped cilantro
- Lime wedges
- Salsa verde or salsa roja

## Instructions

1. **Dry-brine the pork the night before.** Remove any skin and only thick, hard exterior fat from the **4-pound bone-in shoulder**. Keep a thin fat layer and the fat between the muscles. Pat dry. Mix **14 grams kosher salt (about 5 teaspoons Diamond Crystal or 3 teaspoons Morton), 1 teaspoon black pepper, 1 teaspoon Mexican oregano, and the optional 1 teaspoon New Mexico chile (½ teaspoon if hot)**. Rub the full mixture over the pork, including its natural creases. Set it on a rack over a rimmed tray and refrigerate **uncovered for 8–24 hours**, on the bottom shelf away from other food. Without a rack, use a shallow dish that catches the juices. Keep the citrus, water, and lard for the next day. If you have already applied the fuller spice rub from the earlier draft, leave it on and skip this seasoning step; do not add another dose of salt.

2. **Set up the Dutch oven.** The next day, heat the oven to **275°F (135°C)** with a rack in the lower third. Keep the pork refrigerated until you are ready to cook. Do not rinse it or add more salt. Melt **¼ cup lard** in the Dutch oven over low heat. Add **1 quartered medium yellow onion, 6 smashed garlic cloves, ½ cup fresh orange juice, 2 strips orange zest, ¼ cup water, 2 bay leaves, and the optional 1-inch piece of cinnamon stick**. Set the pork in the pot, fat side up. The liquid should cover the bottom in a shallow layer; it does not need to come halfway up the meat. Add **water in ¼-cup increments** only if needed to cover the bottom. Bring the liquid to a gentle simmer and cover tightly. The pork goes in without an initial sear; browning comes after it is tender. Discard the raw juices from the dry-brining tray and wash it before reuse.

3. **Cook gently until the bone releases.** Put the covered pot in the **275°F (135°C)** oven. At about 3 hours, remove the **2 strips orange zest and the optional cinnamon piece** with a slotted spoon. Check that some liquid remains beneath the fat. If the bottom is nearly dry or the onion is catching, carefully add **¼ cup hot water** and cover again. Start testing the meat at about 4½–5 hours. A fork should turn easily in the thickest part, and the bone should pull free with little resistance. A thermometer will often read around **195–205°F (90–96°C)** away from the bone, but tenderness decides when it is ready. Expect about 6 hours. If it still resists, continue covered in 30-minute intervals. If it is tender earlier, take it out.

4. **Rest and separate the fat from the juices.** Lift the pork onto a clean rimmed tray, supporting it with a slotted spoon. Rest for 10–15 minutes. Remove the **2 bay leaves** and any remaining zest or cinnamon. Strain the pot liquid into a heatproof jug and let it settle. Spoon the fat from the top into a separate bowl. Measure **¼ cup of this fat** for crisping; add enough lard to make ¼ cup if needed. Keep the liquid beneath it separately. If those juices taste weak, simmer them for 5–10 minutes to concentrate them, stopping before they become too salty. Measure **¼ cup defatted juices** for the pork. If you have less, add just enough water to reach ¼ cup. Reserve any extra juices for serving.

5. **Pull into large pieces.** Remove the bone and any firm connective tissue. Pull the pork into rough **1–2-inch pieces** with two forks or tongs. Stop before it becomes a pile of fine strands. Divide the pork into **4 roughly equal batches**. Keep the pieces loosely covered while you prepare the skillet.

6. **Crisp in the reserved fat.** Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add **1 tablespoon of the reserved fat**. Toss the first batch of pork with **1 tablespoon of the measured juices**, then spread it in the skillet in a single layer with space between the pieces. Cook undisturbed for **3–4 minutes**, until the underside has a brown crust. Turn the pieces and cook for **1–2 minutes** more. Adjust the heat if the fat smokes or the seasoning darkens too quickly. Repeat with the other **3 batches**, using **1 tablespoon fat and 1 tablespoon juices for each batch**. This uses the full **¼ cup fat and ¼ cup juices**. Aim for one or two browned sides and a soft center. Taste a cooked piece and add **kosher salt only if needed**. Serve each batch promptly for the best texture.

## What Makes the Difference

**Finish cooking before you start crisping.** If the pork fights the fork, it needs more covered cooking. Browning a tough piece will not make it tender. Six hours is a planning estimate, not a required stopping point.

**Keep the fat.** It carries pork flavor and helps brown the surface. Separate it from the watery cooking juices so you can control how much of each goes into the skillet.

**Use only a little juice before crisping.** A crowded skillet or a pool of broth makes the pork steam. This recipe uses just 1 tablespoon of juice per batch. Serve extra juices on the side.

**Keep some large pieces soft inside.** Fine shreds dry quickly. Brown a few surfaces, then stop while the centers are still moist. Add lime at the table for a fresh finish.

The tender-then-brown approach follows the principle in [Rick Bayless's home-style carnitas](https://www.rickbayless.com/recipe/home-style-pork-carnitas/). This recipe adapts that approach for a whole, bone-in shoulder, a longer covered cook, and a skillet finish.

## Serving Suggestions

Serve in warm corn tortillas with diced white onion, cilantro, salsa verde or salsa roja, and lime wedges. Rice and beans make it a full meal. Add lime after crisping so the browned edges stay crisp.

Crisp only the pork you plan to eat now. For a smaller meal, use one of the four batches with **1 tablespoon reserved fat and 1 tablespoon cooking juices**. Keep the other portions for another meal and brown them when you serve them.
