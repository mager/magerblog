When the internet was just getting started in the mid-1990s, I was obsessed with learning HTML. Once I got tired of Geocities, I wanted to host my own website. At age 13, I gathered up all my allowances and sent Network Solutions $100 to register [lespaul.com](http://web.archive.org/web/19990125100817/http://www.lespaul.com/) (and yes, my CDNow ad still renders on archive.org).

![lespaul.com](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMn7au1mfKl_7uKocozvGHdbWk3t4QBPMXEyUHdXqSiL_B5e6kOuQdzW3A2AD_6KllR4oruBkAAIu2XQBoZVQrsZtTV8k2QGjrzpSginORLo1_qUVZe7-xNCUEnK-cAUM7LCxuBqMaDnnRe_tWeCJUSNg=w2320-h1520-s-no-gm)

That site became my sandbox. I was a guitar enthusiast who wanted to catalog every instrument I could find. My favorite was the [Gibson Les Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibson_Les_Paul), so I figured buying lespaul.com was a smart move. I updated it almost every day, learning about guitars, web design, CSS, and publishing along the way.

[I maintained the site for about a year](http://web.archive.org/web/19990202060026/www.lespaul.com/Update/index.htm) until Gibson sent a cease-and-desist letter claiming they had owned the "Les Paul" trademark since 1954. My dad’s lawyer jumped in, explaining I was just a harmless 13-year-old playing with the web. I wasn’t selling anything and I linked to Gibson all over the place.

We eventually reached an agreement—I handed over the domain, and in exchange I received a free Epiphone Les Paul Custom Black Beauty.

Days later, I bought [guitarinfo.com](http://web.archive.org/web/19991023035319/www.guitarinfo.com/about.htm):

![guitarinfo.com](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNnHeWwyjTl0E61-Fij_YMeIUteOkKj4nnn5OdPppYvKmGZYH1K-5nGbH0DmeSworzx-HXr0Hb-cc6hflYxZ8fi0Up9KcuKN1pyRBOMCB33lFQmQs5tKfVKvEeMTV0YEJEMY5ep_Og76STwhgE1_69M9Q=w2320-h1520-s-no-gm)

Twelve years later, I still wonder if Les Paul himself ever knew I owned his name dot com. I liked to imagine he told Gibson, "Let’s get this ugly website redirecting to something prettier."
