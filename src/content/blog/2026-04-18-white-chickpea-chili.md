<div class="recipe-intro">

This starts with the Live Eat Learn [white chickpea chili](https://www.liveeatlearn.com/white-chickpea-chili/) idea and keeps the spirit intact: pantry beans, green chiles, a fast one-pot build, and a creamy finish. I skipped the dairy, used a little coconut cream, and still blended part of the pot so the beans do most of the thickening work. The broth gets silky, the coconut cream rounds it out without taking over, and it still lands squarely in white chili territory. Hearty enough to feel like dinner, easy enough to throw together without thinking too hard.

</div>

#### Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 teaspoon butter
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon flour
- 2 teaspoons cumin
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 teaspoon oregano
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 cup vegetable broth
- 1/2 to 1 cup coconut cream
- 2 cans chickpeas, drained
- 2 cans white beans, drained (navy or cannellini both work)
- 1 can corn, drained
- 2 small cans diced green chiles
- Optional toppings: tortilla chips, Monterey Jack, avocado, cilantro

</div>

<div class="recipe-instructions">

Heat the olive oil and butter in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, garlic, and green chiles and cook until the onion softens, about 3 minutes.

Add the flour, cumin, smoked paprika, oregano, salt, and pepper. Stir until the flour disappears into the onions and everything smells like you're heading toward something good.

Pour in the broth, then add the chickpeas, white beans, and corn. Stir well and bring it to a gentle simmer. With less liquid, this will tighten up fast, so 10 minutes is usually enough to get everything cozy and concentrated.

Stir in the coconut cream, then blend a portion of the chili until it turns creamy and thickened. An immersion blender is easiest, but scooping out a cup or two and blending that separately works too. You don't need to puree the whole pot, just rough up enough beans to give it body. If it gets too thick, add a splash more broth.

Serve with crushed tortilla chips, shredded cheese, avocado, cilantro, or whatever version of "something on top" sounds right that night.

#### Variations

If you want to take this in a Thai direction, swap the cumin and oregano for a spoonful of green curry paste, then finish with extra coconut cream, lime, and cilantro. If you want it to lean Indian, trade the oregano for coriander and turmeric, add a little ginger, and finish with garam masala and lemon. Same structure, different passport.
