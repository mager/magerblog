## The Problem

Right now, if you want to give your AI agent a new skill, your options are:

1. Write a prompt yourself and hope it works
2. Find something on a developer-focused registry like skills.sh
3. Copy-paste from a Reddit thread

Option 1 requires you to be good at prompt engineering. Option 2 requires you to be comfortable with GitHub. Option 3 is chaos.

None of these work for the pastry chef who has 20 years of sourdough knowledge. Or the music teacher who knows exactly how to explain chord progressions. Or the retired professor with a research methodology that changed how her students think.

**The people with the most valuable knowledge are the least likely to be on GitHub.**

## The Vision

[Loooom](https://loooom.xyz) is a social skills marketplace. Here's what that means:

**Skills are what you know, made installable.** A skill on Loooom is a structured document — written in markdown, following the open [AgentSkills.io](https://agentskills.io) spec — that teaches an AI agent how to do something the way YOU do it. Not a generic prompt. Your actual methodology, your frameworks, your taste, your experience.

**Anyone can create one.** No coding required. No git. Just a simple markdown editor where you write what you know. If you can write a recipe, you can write a skill.

**Authors are real people.** Verified profiles. You know exactly who created the skill you're installing. When you install a songwriting skill, you can see that it was written by someone who's actually written songs — not an anonymous prompt engineer.

**Skills are always free.** This is non-negotiable. Knowledge wants to be free. But great work deserves recognition, so authors can receive donations from people who love their skills. We're still figuring out the exact mechanism, but the principle is set: **never a paywall, always a tip jar.**

## Who's It For?

The fun answer: everyone. The real answer: anyone who knows something worth sharing.

Imagine:

- **Dave Grohl** sharing skills for drumming fills AND writing lyrics from the gut
- **A Bon Appétit writer** sharing their recipe development process
- **A manga artist** teaching panel composition and visual storytelling
- **A startup founder** packaging their fundraising framework
- **An herbalist** documenting plant medicine and tincture recipes
- **Your favorite professor** finally making their teaching methodology available to everyone

The common thread: these are people with deep expertise who currently have no good way to make that expertise available to AI agents. Loooom gives them a place.

## Historical Skills

Here's something fun we're doing: the Loooom community account creates "historical" skills — educational, clearly disclaimed, but genuinely useful.

What does Plato's Socratic questioning method look like as an installable skill? We wrote it. It teaches an agent to never give answers, only ask questions. To find contradictions gently. To admit its own ignorance.

What about Lincoln's speechwriting? We broke down the Gettysburg Address into a framework: start with shared ground, elevate the stakes, use the rule of threes, make it personal then universal, end with a call forward.

These historical skills serve two purposes: they show what's possible on the platform, and they're actually useful. Donations to historical skills fund the Loooom community.

## The Tech (For the Nerds)

- **SvelteKit 5 + TypeScript** — fast, minimal, great DX
- **Neon Postgres** — relational data for a social platform
- **Content-addressed versioning** — every skill version is a sha256 hash of its contents. Immutable, verifiable, no git complexity
- **AgentSkills.io spec** — agent-agnostic. Works with Claude, GPT, Gemini, local models, whatever
- **Vercel** — auto-deploys from main
- **Open source** — [github.com/mager/loooom.xyz](https://github.com/mager/loooom.xyz)

## The Name

Loooom. Four o's. Like extra yarn on the spool.

A loom is where threads become fabric. Loooom is where knowledge becomes skills. The whole brand leans into craft — yarn, weaving, handwriting, maker culture. The site should feel like the hot girl at the coffee shop who has a book and probably likes anime. Not a SaaS landing page.
