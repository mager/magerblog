I picked up Ancient Grain Pizza Dough at Whole Foods, a can of pizza sauce, a mozzarella ball, and pepperoni. This is my same-day version for a 10 × 14-inch pan, with a full pound of mozzarella. I want to see how far one bag stretches and how crisp I can get the bottom. Next time I’ll [make the dough from scratch](/blog/2026-11-07-detroit-style-pizza/).

**Working draft.** The amounts, temperature, and timing are my starting points for the first bake. Allow about 3½ hours from fridge to table, mostly for the dough to warm and rise.

## Ingredients

- **1 pound (454 g) refrigerated Whole Foods Ancient Grain Pizza Dough**, one full bag if the label says 16 ounces
- **2 tablespoons refined canola oil**, for the pan
- **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella from a ball**
- **2 ounces (55 g) sliced pepperoni**, about **½ cup loosely packed**
- **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**

## Schedule for a 5:30 p.m. dinner

1. **9:30 a.m. — Check the pan.** Keep the dough and mozzarella in the fridge. If the pan is new bare blue steel, follow its maker's first-use seasoning instructions this morning and let the pan cool before adding dough. If it is already seasoned, check that the inside is clean and dry. The standard Lloyd Detroit pan is coated aluminum and needs an initial wash and thorough drying. Check the oven temperature limit for your pan. [LloydPans first-use guidance](https://lloydpans.com/detroit-style-pizza-pans.html) · [Bare steel pan guidance](https://detroitstylepizza.com/blogs/detroit-style-pizza-blog/bare-seasoned-steel-authentic-detroit-style-pizza-pans-8x10-and-10x14)
2. **2 p.m. — Start the dough.** Spread **2 tablespoons refined canola oil** over the bottom and lower sides of the cool pan. Add **1 pound (454 g) dough**, turn it once to coat, and gently press it into a rough rectangle. Cover and leave it at room temperature for **45 minutes**. In a cold kitchen, start 30 minutes earlier.
3. **2:45 p.m. — Stretch to the corners.** With oiled fingertips, push the dough outward from the center. If it shrinks back, cover it and wait **15–20 minutes** before trying again. Repeat as needed. Once it fills the corners, cover and let it rise until puffy with a few bubbles near the edges. Start checking after **45 minutes**. If it is ready early, bake early.
4. **4:10 p.m. — Heat the oven and prepare the cheese.** Set a rack in the lower third. Heat the oven to **475°F (245°C)** if your pan permits it. If the mozzarella came packed in liquid, drain it first. Grate **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella** using the large holes of a box grater. If the cheese is too soft to grate, cut it into small cubes. If the cheese feels wet, pat it dry. Spread it on a quarter sheet pan and refrigerate until needed. Set aside **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**. Keep it in the fridge until the pizza goes into the oven.
5. **4:55 p.m. — Bake the crust first.** When the dough is puffy, uncover the pan and bake the plain crust for **8–10 minutes**, until the surface looks dry and set with little color. This gives the base a head start before the toppings go on.
6. **Top and finish baking.** Scatter **2 ounces (55 g, about ½ cup) sliced pepperoni** over the crust, then spread all **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella** in an even layer. Bring the cheese all the way to the pan walls so it can brown along the edges. Bake for **10–15 minutes more**. Check for bubbling cheese, browned edges, and a firm golden underside. Use a heat-safe spatula to lift a corner and check the bottom. While it bakes, warm **¾ cup canned pizza sauce** in a small saucepan. If the sauce runs easily off a spoon, simmer it until it holds in a stripe.
7. **Rest and serve.** Loosen the edges with a spatula suitable for your pan and lift the pizza onto a wire rack. Rest for **5 minutes**. Spoon the warm sauce over the pizza in **three stripes of about ¼ cup each**. Cut into **8 squares**.

For this first bake, I will record the bag weight, how long it takes to fill the corners, and the color of the bottom. [King Arthur's weeknight Detroit pizza](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/weeknight-detroit-pizza-recipe) uses a plain-crust first bake and is the starting point for that step.
