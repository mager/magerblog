I picked up Ancient Grain Pizza Dough at Whole Foods, a can of pizza sauce, a mozzarella ball, and pepperoni. This is my same-day version for a 10 × 14-inch pan, with a full pound of mozzarella and white cheddar around the edges. I’m starting with the whole bag, about 22 ounces, to see how it fills the pan and how crisp I can get the bottom. Next time I’ll [make the dough from scratch](/blog/2026-11-07-detroit-style-pizza/).

**Working draft.** The amounts, temperature, and timing are my starting points for the first bake. Allow about 3½ hours from fridge to table, mostly for the dough to warm and rise.

## Ingredients

- **About 22 ounces (624 g) refrigerated Whole Foods Ancient Grain Pizza Dough** for a thicker crust, or **16 ounces (454 g)** for a thinner crust
- **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil**, for the pan
- **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella from a ball**
- **4 ounces (113 g) white cheddar**, shredded
- **2 ounces (55 g) sliced pepperoni**, about **½ cup loosely packed**
- **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**
- **1 garlic clove, lightly crushed, and 2 small sprigs fresh thyme**, optional, for the sauce

## Schedule for a 5:30 p.m. dinner

1. **9:30 a.m. — Check the pan.** Keep the dough and mozzarella in the fridge. If the pan is new bare blue steel, follow its maker's first-use seasoning instructions this morning and let the pan cool before adding dough. If it is already seasoned, check that the inside is clean and dry. The standard Lloyd Detroit pan is coated aluminum and needs an initial wash and thorough drying. Check the oven temperature limit for your pan. [LloydPans first-use guidance](https://lloydpans.com/detroit-style-pizza-pans.html) · [Bare steel pan guidance](https://detroitstylepizza.com/blogs/detroit-style-pizza-blog/bare-seasoned-steel-authentic-detroit-style-pizza-pans-8x10-and-10x14)
2. **2 p.m. — Start the dough.** Spread **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil** over the bottom and lower sides of the cool pan. Add **about 22 ounces (624 g) dough** for a thicker crust or **16 ounces (454 g)** for a thinner crust. Place the dough directly in the oiled pan so the bottom can brown. Turn the dough once to coat it and gently press it into a rough rectangle. Cover the pan and leave it at room temperature for **45–60 minutes**. In a cold kitchen, start 30 minutes earlier.
3. **2:45–3 p.m. — Stretch to the corners.** With oiled fingertips, push the dough outward from the center. If it shrinks back, cover it and wait **15–20 minutes** before trying again. Repeat as needed. Once it fills the corners, cover and let it rise until puffy with a few bubbles near the edges. Set a timer for **50 minutes** to check the rise. If the dough still looks flat or feels tight, cover it again and check after **20–30 minutes**.
4. **4:10 p.m. — Heat the oven and prepare the cheese.** Set a rack in the lower third. Heat the oven to **475°F (245°C)** if your pan permits it. If the mozzarella came packed in liquid, drain it first. Grate **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella** using the large holes of a box grater. If the cheese is too soft to grate, cut it into small cubes. If the cheese feels wet, pat it dry. Spread it on a quarter sheet pan. Grate **4 ounces (113 g) white cheddar** and refrigerate both cheeses until needed. Set aside **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**. Keep it in the fridge until the pizza goes into the oven.
5. **4:55 p.m. — Bake the crust first.** When the dough is puffy, uncover the pan and put it in the oven. Check the plain crust after **8 minutes**. Bake until the surface looks dry and set with little color. The thicker crust may need a few more minutes. This gives the base a head start before the toppings go on.
6. **Top and finish baking.** Scatter **2 ounces (55 g, about ½ cup) sliced pepperoni** over the crust, then spread all **16 ounces (454 g) mozzarella** in an even layer over the pepperoni. Add all **4 ounces (113 g) shredded white cheddar** along the four pan walls so it browns into a crisp edge. Bake for **10–15 minutes more**. Check for bubbling cheese, browned edges, and a firm golden underside. Use a heat-safe spatula to lift a corner and check the bottom. While it bakes, warm **¾ cup canned pizza sauce** in a small saucepan. If using the garlic and thyme, add **1 lightly crushed garlic clove and 2 small sprigs of fresh thyme**. Simmer gently for **5–10 minutes**, then remove the garlic and sprigs. If the sauce runs easily off a spoon, simmer it until it holds in a stripe.
7. **Rest and serve.** Run a thin, heat-safe spatula suitable for your pan around all four edges. Use a wide spatula to slide the pizza onto a wire rack. Rest for **5 minutes**. Spoon the warm sauce over the pizza in **three stripes of about ¼ cup each**. Cut into **8 squares**.

For this first bake, I will record the bag weight, how long it takes to fill the corners, and the color of the bottom. [King Arthur's weeknight Detroit pizza](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/weeknight-detroit-pizza-recipe) uses a plain-crust first bake and is the starting point for that step.
