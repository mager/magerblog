I lived in the Detroit area from 2017 to 2019, and Jet's and Buddy's are the inspiration for this pizza. For my same-day version, I picked up pizza dough at Whole Foods, a can of pizza sauce, a mozzarella ball, and pepperoni. I use a 10 × 14-inch pan, the whole bag of dough (about 22 ounces), 12 ounces of mozzarella, and white cheddar around the edges.

For my second bake, I used regular pizza dough with pepperoni, thinly sliced red onion, and chopped olives. The hero and step photos are from this bake; the [first bake photos](#first-bake-photos) are below.

If you want to make your own dough, the [overnight homemade dough option](#homemade-dough-option) below includes the formula and proofing schedule from my original draft. I have not tested that dough yet; the photos here show the store-bought version.

Allow about 3½ hours from fridge to table. The prep time includes the dough rests and oven preheat. Allow about **21–23 minutes of baking at 450°F convection**: **8 minutes** for the bare crust, then **13–15 minutes after the mozzarella goes on**. The schedule below is for a 5:30 p.m. dinner.

## Ingredients

- **About 22 ounces (624 g) refrigerated store-bought pizza dough** for a thicker crust, or **16 ounces (454 g)** for a thinner crust
- **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil**, for the pan
- **12 ounces (340 g) mozzarella from a ball**
- **4 ounces (113 g) white cheddar**, shredded
- **2 ounces (55 g) sliced pepperoni**, about **½ cup loosely packed**
- **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**
- **1 garlic clove, lightly crushed, and 2 small sprigs fresh thyme**, optional, for the sauce

## Method

1. **9:30 a.m. — Check the pan.**

   Keep the dough and mozzarella in the fridge. If the pan is new bare blue steel, follow its maker's first-use seasoning instructions this morning and let the pan cool before adding dough. If it is already seasoned, check that the inside is clean and dry.

   The standard Lloyd Detroit pan is coated aluminum and needs an initial wash and thorough drying. Check the oven temperature limit for your pan.

   [LloydPans first-use guidance](https://lloydpans.com/detroit-style-pizza-pans.html) · [Bare steel pan guidance](https://detroitstylepizza.com/blogs/detroit-style-pizza-blog/bare-seasoned-steel-authentic-detroit-style-pizza-pans-8x10-and-10x14)

2. **2 p.m. — Start the dough.**

   Spread **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil** over the bottom and lower sides of the cool pan. Add **about 22 ounces (624 g) dough** for a thicker crust or **16 ounces (454 g)** for a thinner crust. Place the dough directly in the oiled pan so the bottom can brown.

   Turn the dough once to coat it and gently press it into a rough rectangle. Cover the pan and leave it at room temperature for **45–60 minutes**. In a cold kitchen, start 30 minutes earlier.

3. **2:45–3 p.m. — Stretch to the corners.**

   With oiled fingertips, push the dough outward from the center. If it shrinks back, cover it and wait **15–20 minutes** before trying again. Repeat as needed.

   Once it fills the corners, cover and let it rise until puffy with a few bubbles near the edges. Set a timer for **50 minutes** to check the rise. If the dough still looks flat or feels tight, cover it again and check after **20–30 minutes**.

4. **4:10 p.m. — Heat the oven and prepare the cheese.**

   Set a rack in the lower third. Heat the oven to **450°F (232°C) convection** if your pan permits it.

   If the mozzarella came packed in liquid, drain it first. Grate **12 ounces (340 g) mozzarella** using the large holes of a box grater. If the cheese is too soft to grate, cut it into small cubes. If the cheese feels wet, pat it dry. Spread it on a quarter sheet pan.

   Grate **4 ounces (113 g) white cheddar** and refrigerate both cheeses until needed.

   Set aside **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**. Keep it in the fridge until the pizza goes into the oven.

5. **4:55 p.m. — Prebake the crust.**

   Bake the dough on its own for **8 minutes**, without pepperoni, cheese, or sauce.

   The surface should look set before you add the toppings. If it still looks wet or doughy, give it **2 more minutes** and check again.

6. **Add the pepperoni and cheese, then bake.**

   Scatter **2 ounces (55 g, about ½ cup) sliced pepperoni** over the prebaked crust.

   Spread **12 ounces (340 g) grated mozzarella** in an even layer over the pepperoni. Add **4 ounces (113 g) shredded white cheddar** along all four pan walls.

   Bake for **7–9 minutes**, checking at **7 minutes**. The mozzarella should be fully melted and starting to brown before you add the sauce.

   While it bakes, warm **¾ cup canned pizza sauce**. If using the garlic and thyme, add **1 lightly crushed garlic clove and 2 small sprigs of fresh thyme**, simmer gently for **5–10 minutes**, then remove the garlic and sprigs. If the sauce runs easily off a spoon, simmer it until it holds in a stripe.

7. **Add the sauce and finish baking.**

   Spoon the warm **¾ cup pizza sauce** over the cheese in three stripes of about **¼ cup each**. Leave the cheddar edges exposed.

   Bake for about **6 more minutes**, until the underside is firm and golden. Lift a corner with a heat-safe spatula to check it. That makes **13–15 minutes total after the mozzarella goes on**.

8. **Rest and serve.**

   Run a thin, heat-safe spatula suitable for your pan around all four edges. Slide the pizza onto a wire rack and rest it for **5 minutes**.

   Transfer it to a cutting board and cut it into **8 squares**.

## Homemade dough option

This is the dough formula I plan to test in the same **10 × 14-inch pan**. It replaces the store-bought dough and its warming and proofing steps. The cheese, sauce, and topping amounts above stay the same. **I have not baked this homemade version yet**, so the baking times in the main method are a starting point for this dough.

Allow **24–48 hours in the fridge**, followed by about **3–4 hours to warm, stretch, and rise in the pan**. Including mixing, baking, and resting, that is roughly **29–54 hours from start to serving**. The prep time at the top of this page is for the store-bought version.

For Saturday dinner, mix on Friday afternoon and refrigerate by about 2 p.m. Take it out at 2 p.m. Saturday and aim to bake around 6 p.m. For the longer rest, mix on Thursday instead. A cool kitchen can add time; the dough's rise matters more than the clock.

### Dough ingredients

- **400 g bread flour**, about **3⅓ cups**, spooned into the cup and leveled
- **280 g water**, about **1 cup plus 3 tablespoons**, at cool room temperature
- **8 g fine sea salt**, about **1⅓ teaspoons**; use the weight if substituting kosher salt
- **1.5 g instant yeast**, about **½ teaspoon**
- **8 g olive oil**, about **2 teaspoons**, for the dough
- **1 teaspoon olive oil**, for the dough container
- **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil**, for the pan; this is the same pan oil listed above, not an additional amount

The formula is **70% hydration**: 280 grams of water for 400 grams of flour. Salt and olive oil are each 2% of the flour weight; instant yeast is 0.375%. The mixed dough weighs about **698 grams**, a little more than the store-bought bag. Oil for the container and pan is separate.

Use bread flour and weigh the ingredients if possible. The cup and spoon amounts are approximate. This is a soft, sticky dough; do not add flour just to make it feel like a dry dough ball.

### Mix, refrigerate, and proof

1. **Mix the dough.** In a large bowl, stir together **400 g bread flour (about 3⅓ cups), 8 g fine sea salt (about 1⅓ teaspoons), and 1.5 g instant yeast (about ½ teaspoon)**. Add **280 g water (about 1 cup plus 3 tablespoons)** and **8 g olive oil (about 2 teaspoons)**. Mix with a sturdy spoon until no dry flour remains. Scrape down the bowl, cover, and rest for **20 minutes**.

2. **Fold three times.** Wet your fingers, lift one side of the dough, and fold it over the center. Turn the bowl and repeat on the other three sides. This is one set. Cover and rest for **20 minutes**, then do a second set. Rest another **20 minutes**, then do a third set. Stretch only as far as the dough allows without tearing; do not knead in extra flour.

3. **Refrigerate for 24–48 hours.** Coat a covered food container with **1 teaspoon olive oil**. Transfer the dough, turn it once to coat, and close the lid. Use a container with room for the dough to double. Refrigerate after the last fold. Some bubbles and expansion are expected, but it does not need to double in the fridge. For a first batch, start with **24 hours** so there is a clear comparison for the next bake.

4. **Oil the pan and start the final rise.** About **4 hours before baking**, spread **2 tablespoons refined avocado oil or refined canola oil** across the bottom and lower sides of the cool pan. Transfer the full batch of cold dough to the pan. Turn it once in the oil, then gently press it into a broad rectangle. Cover without pressing the cover onto the dough and leave it for **45–60 minutes**. It probably will not reach the corners yet.

5. **Stretch, then let it rise.** Gently press the dough outward with your fingertips. If it pulls back, cover it and wait **20 minutes** before trying again. Once it reaches the corners, leave it covered until visibly puffy, with bubbles near the edges and a slight wobble when the pan moves. Allow about **3–4 hours total from fridge to oven**, including the previous rest. In a cool room, allow longer. Do not flatten the dough again after this rise.

6. **Join the main method at step 4.** During the last part of the rise, heat the oven to **450°F (232°C) convection** and prepare the cheese and sauce as described above. Then prebake the bare crust, add the toppings, and finish baking. Check that the crust is set before topping and that the underside is firm and golden before removing the pizza; this larger homemade batch may need more time.

### Notes for the homemade batch

- **Dough keeps shrinking:** Give it another covered rest before stretching. Forcing it into the corners can tear it.
- **Dense center:** Check whether the dough was visibly puffy before topping. Note the room temperature and final rise time before changing the formula.
- **Gummy layer under the cheese:** Check the bake and sauce thickness. Extra toppings or a pale base can make the center harder to cook through.
- **Pale bottom with dark cheese:** Try a lower rack first on the next bake.
- **Dough rises and then collapses:** Record the fridge rest and room-temperature rise, then shorten the relevant stage next time.

For my first homemade batch, I want notes on the flour brand, fridge time, final rise, oven rack, and bake time. Then I can compare a 24-hour dough with a 48-hour dough while keeping the rest the same.
