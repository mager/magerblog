<div class="recipe-intro">

Creamed spinach is the side I always order at a steakhouse and almost never make at home, because the restaurant version usually involves an alarming amount of heavy cream. This is my fix: a roux of butter, flour, and milk does most of the structural work, so a modest pour of cream and a little cream cheese can stay for richness without taking over. Heavy on garlic, heavier on spinach, finished with parmesan and nutmeg. It eats like the indulgent classic but doesn't sit on you like a brick next to a ribeye.

</div>

<div class="recipe-body">
<div class="recipe-ingredients">

#### Spinach

- 1.5 lbs fresh spinach (about three 5 oz bags, or two large bunches), or 1 lb frozen chopped spinach, thawed
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt (for wilting)

#### Garlic cream sauce

- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 small yellow onion or large shallot, finely diced
- 8 cloves garlic, minced (yes, eight)
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1.5 cups whole milk, warmed
- 1/3 cup heavy cream
- 2 oz cream cheese, cubed
- 1/2 cup grated parmesan, plus more for finishing
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 1 tsp kosher salt, to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional)

</div>

<div class="recipe-instructions">

Wilt the spinach. If using fresh, heat the olive oil in a large pot or deep skillet over medium-high. Add the spinach in batches with a teaspoon of salt, tossing with tongs, adding more as each handful collapses. It cooks down dramatically — 1.5 lbs becomes about 1.5 cups cooked. This takes 4–6 minutes total. (For frozen, skip straight to draining.)

Squeeze it dry. This is the step that separates good creamed spinach from a watery puddle. Dump the wilted spinach into a colander and press hard with the back of a spoon, then let it cool enough to handle and squeeze it in your hands or twist it in a clean towel until almost no liquid comes out. Roughly chop the dried spinach and set aside.

Build the base. Wipe out the pot and melt the butter over medium heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and translucent, about 4–5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook 1–2 minutes more, just until fragrant — don't let it brown or it turns bitter.

Make the roux. Sprinkle the flour over the onion and garlic and stir constantly for about 1 minute to cook off the raw flour taste. It will look like wet sand.

Whisk in the milk. Pour the warm milk in slowly, whisking the whole time to keep it smooth. Bring it to a gentle simmer and let it thicken for 2–3 minutes until it coats the back of a spoon.

Enrich it. Lower the heat. Stir in the cream and the cubed cream cheese, whisking until the cream cheese fully melts in. Add the parmesan, nutmeg, salt, plenty of black pepper, and the red pepper flakes if using. Taste and adjust — it should be assertively seasoned, since the spinach is about to dilute it.

Fold in the spinach. Add the chopped spinach and stir until every bit is coated and heated through, 2–3 minutes. If it's tighter than you like, loosen with a splash of milk; if it's loose, simmer another minute.

Finish and serve. Kill the heat, taste one more time for salt and nutmeg, and shower with extra parmesan. Serve hot alongside steak, roast chicken, or honestly a spoon.

#### Notes and Tips

- The squeeze is non-negotiable. Skipping it is the #1 reason homemade creamed spinach turns soupy.
- Eight cloves reads like a lot until you taste it — gently sweated garlic mellows into background sweetness, not a punch. Use more if your house runs like mine.
- Want it lighter still? Drop the cream cheese and bump the milk by a couple tablespoons; you'll lose a little body but keep the flavor.
- Make-ahead: this reheats well over low heat with a splash of milk. The texture holds for a couple days in the fridge.
- Frozen spinach works great here and is faster — just thaw fully and squeeze it even harder than fresh.
