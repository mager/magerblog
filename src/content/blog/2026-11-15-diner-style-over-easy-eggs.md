The diner egg I want has a tender white, pale edges, and a thin white film over the yolk. When I cut into it, the yolk should run into the toast. A quick flip sets the white on top. A lid and a little water can give a similar finish with the yolk facing up.

This is a working draft. Use the times as starting points and watch the whites as they cook.

## Ingredients

- 2 large pasteurized shell eggs
- 1 teaspoon neutral oil, such as canola or avocado oil
- 1 pinch fine salt
- 1 teaspoon water, only for the covered method

## Instructions

1. **Heat the surface.** Set a seasoned flat top or a nonstick skillet over medium-low heat. Spread **1 teaspoon neutral oil** into a thin layer with no puddles. The oil should not smoke.
2. **Set the whites.** Crack the **2 eggs** into separate small bowls. Slide them onto the surface with space between them. Cook for **2 to 4 minutes**, until the whites are opaque almost to the yolks and a thin spatula can lift each egg in one piece. If the edges sputter or brown before the whites set, lower the heat.
3. **Flip and finish.** Slide the spatula under each egg far enough to support the yolk. Turn each egg gently and start checking after **10 seconds**. Take it off as soon as the white is fully set. Aim for **10 to 20 seconds** on the second side. Divide **1 pinch fine salt** between the eggs and serve immediately.

## For the thin white film without a flip

After step 2, add **1 teaspoon water** to a bare spot beside the eggs, away from any oil that has collected. Cover at once with a lid or griddle dome. Check after **15 seconds**, then every **5 to 10 seconds**. As soon as a thin white film covers the yolks and no clear white remains, remove the eggs from the heat. Divide **1 pinch fine salt** between them and serve immediately.

This is [steam-basting](https://www.incredibleegg.org/recipes/cooking-school/how-to-make-fried-eggs/). Keep the lid on only until the film forms and the whites set. More time under the lid will firm the yolks.

Use pasteurized shell eggs for the runny yolks in this recipe. The [FDA's egg safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/82227/download) recommends pasteurized eggs for recipes served with undercooked eggs.
