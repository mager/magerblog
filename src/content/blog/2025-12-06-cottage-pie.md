<div class="recipe-intro">

I adapted this cottage pie from Alton Brown's shepherd's pie recipe. This version uses one 1-pound package of ground beef or turkey, with the filling and potato topping adjusted for the smaller batch. I'm using turkey this time; the turkey instructions are below. Frozen corn, peas, and carrots keep the preparation simple.

</div>

<div class="recipe-body">
<div class="recipe-ingredients">

#### For the Filling

- 1 pound ground beef or ground turkey (see turkey instructions below)
- 1½ tablespoons vegetable oil (or canola oil)
- ⅔ cup chopped onion
- 1 cup frozen corn, peas, and carrots mix
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- ¾ teaspoon kosher salt, divided
- ¼ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
- 1½ teaspoons curry powder (optional)
- 1½ teaspoons chopped fresh rosemary
- ¾ teaspoon chopped fresh thyme
- 1½ tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 whole tube tomato paste
- ⅓ cup red wine
- 1 cup unsalted chicken stock or bone broth
- ¾ teaspoon Worcestershire sauce

#### For the Potatoes

- 1½ pounds russet potatoes
- ⅓ cup half-and-half
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, cut in cubes
- 1 large egg yolk
- ¾ cup shredded cheddar cheese (about 3 ounces), divided
- ½ teaspoon kosher salt, plus more for the cooking water
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

</div>

<div class="recipe-instructions">

**Prep first:** Peel and dice the 1½ pounds potatoes. Measure ⅔ cup chopped onion, 1½ teaspoons chopped fresh rosemary, and ¾ teaspoon chopped fresh thyme. Mince the 3 cloves garlic. Shred ¾ cup cheddar cheese (about 3 ounces) and divide into two portions of 6 tablespoons each. Set aside one portion for the top. Measure out the other ingredients.

Put the 1½ pounds diced potatoes in a pot and cover with cold water by about 1 inch. Salt the water generously. Bring to a boil over **high heat**, then reduce to **medium-low**, adjusting as needed to keep a gentle boil. Cook for about **15-20 minutes after the water starts boiling**, aiming for an internal temperature of **about 210°F (99°C)**. Lift a large piece out with a slotted spoon and insert an instant-read thermometer into its center. Check a few large pieces; the probe should slide in easily. This is the potato temperature, not the water temperature. At high elevations, boiling water is cooler, so use tenderness as the final check instead of waiting for 210°F. See [ThermoWorks' potato temperature guide](https://blog.thermoworks.com/the-dirt-on-potatoes/) for the temperature and altitude details.

Drain the cooked potatoes well and return them to the warm pot, off the heat. Now preheat the oven to **400°F** and start the filling. You can mash the potatoes while the vegetables and meat cook.

Heat 1½ tablespoons oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add ⅔ cup chopped onion and cook for 5-10 minutes until softened and starting to brown. Add 1 cup frozen vegetables (corn, peas, carrots) and cook for **at least 10 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until hot throughout and tender and the released water has mostly evaporated. Stir in the 3 minced garlic cloves and cook for about 30 seconds before adding the meat.

**Mash while the filling cooks:** Mash the potatoes while still hot with ⅓ cup warmed half-and-half and 4 tablespoons butter. Season with ½ teaspoon kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste. Stir in 1 large egg yolk and 6 tablespoons shredded cheddar (about 1½ ounces). Stir the skillet mixture as needed between mashing, then set the finished potatoes aside until assembly.

![Filling](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPJN88jigLaN4t4M0buK_P-Gw1yhkm2L18b7E-qOYbuVTw9S6eR4ziJ05alpfAZqPXSQZTbkTgpRgx19AZhQx_ti9U3la9BEfdHah3ChcRcvwcMKG5A_lrtEiTry6LbNwZc6nftA3Jq3sk94EVISghvQw=w1350-h1350-s-no-gm)

Add 1 pound ground beef, ½ teaspoon kosher salt, and ¼ teaspoon freshly ground black pepper. Cook, breaking up clumps, until the beef is browned and cooked through, about 5-7 minutes. For ground turkey, follow the turkey instructions below for this step and draining.

![Browning the beef](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczM1PMLVQpVtkF1Kest44y6M9XmGwfTc645T4vRXBkM0wnsiNpS3Ln6ztmUCq-dsuV-oalEPWqprLKWbRX94n8yCxZHpKUshE9N33vMRzmrgNkW2SNKbYLOMD3I9RsdCR6dFIr5CAXpliPS4SAjJZq-bOA=w2026-h1520-s-no-gm)

Turn off the heat. If there is excess fat, spoon it into a heatproof bowl and let it cool before disposal. For lean turkey, skip this step unless fat has pooled in the pan.

Turn the heat back on to medium. Add ⅓ cup red wine and cook, scraping up any brown bits, for 1-2 minutes until reduced by about half.

Stir in 1½ teaspoons curry powder (if using), 1½ teaspoons rosemary, ¾ teaspoon thyme, the remaining ¼ teaspoon kosher salt for the filling, and 1 whole tube tomato paste. Sprinkle 1½ tablespoons flour over the meat mixture and stir well. Cook for 3-4 minutes.

![Adding spices and flour](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNNLIPmhLPOKEB8ekWbf4E8OM4aezNg9qLShy-xLcr47ERjJwAX3divTncMvlT9snXeOvIdqcRKfxqPrdBqSYeY28Ri-LXOqppNW4nqJK6LNpOuXmt4QuucUmUCAYoND-eHylVx4gwW7q5ptLqRLMpc-Q=w2026-h1520-s-no-gm)

Stir in 1 cup stock (or bone broth) and ¾ teaspoon Worcestershire sauce. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer uncovered, stirring occasionally, until the filling is moist but thick enough to hold the potato layer. Start checking after 10 minutes; keep simmering if it is still runny.

Transfer the meat mixture to a rectangular baking pan. Spread all of the mashed potato mixture evenly over the filling, smoothing it with a spatula. Sprinkle the remaining 6 tablespoons shredded cheddar (about 1½ ounces) on top.

![Topping with potatoes](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNF6w69uYiCsH0u8YT1U1_9RKnkIL-wWFKtpIxRMjGdCgwz_0AQGSv-MGYQTkf5R5h20NzlfiMzEW6kakD6TAV9VdhK6utSRdQl47o2wtbbdr3vaxTj9B43SXDUy69bTmdnkzNDaVyo6qqfHgZ_1Sv2-g=w1350-h1350-s-no-gm)

Bake for 25-30 minutes, or until the topping is lightly browned and the filling is bubbling around the edges. Let it cool for 10-15 minutes before serving.

## Ground turkey variation

For a turkey version, use **1 pound ground turkey thigh** if you can find it. Ground turkey breast also works, but it is leaner and can dry out more easily. The filling and topping above are already sized for 1 pound of meat.

Use the listed **1½ tablespoons vegetable oil** to cook the vegetables, then add **1 pound ground turkey**, **½ teaspoon kosher salt**, and **¼ teaspoon black pepper** in place of the beef step. Break up the turkey and cook it through; do not keep cooking just to get the same dark browning as beef. Skip draining unless there is excess fat in the pan.

Continue with the same filling and potato topping. Bake as directed and check that the turkey filling reaches **165°F (74°C)** with a food thermometer, the [USDA temperature for ground poultry dishes](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-do-I-safely-handle-ground-poultry).

## Make ahead for after the game

**To refrigerate the filling and potatoes before baking:** Transfer the cooked filling and finished mashed potatoes to separate shallow containers. Let both cool briefly for about **15-20 minutes before putting them in the fridge**, then loosely cover and refrigerate. **Do not wait for them to cool completely on the counter. Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking**, or within 1 hour if the room is above 90°F (32°C). Small portions can go into the fridge while still warm; see the [USDA's cooling guidance](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/Can-you-put-hot-food-in-the-refrigerator). Once chilled, cover tightly. Keep the reserved **6 tablespoons cheddar (about 1½ ounces)** refrigerated separately. When ready, assemble as directed, add that cheddar, and bake at **400°F** until browned and the center reaches **165°F (74°C)**. A cold pie will need longer than the usual 25-30 minutes.

You can bake this in the morning and reheat it when you get home from the game. Bake fully as directed, then let it cool briefly for about **15-20 minutes**. For faster cooling, divide it into shallow oven-safe containers, loosely cover with foil, and refrigerate. Metal containers work well for reheating straight from the fridge. **Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking**; do not wait for the pie to cool completely on the counter. Once chilled, seal the foil tightly. These cooling steps follow the [FDA's guidance for leftovers](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/safe-food-handling).

When you get home, preheat the oven to **350°F**. Reheat the refrigerated portions covered with foil. Start checking after **30 minutes** and continue until the center and several other spots reach **165°F (74°C)**, the [USDA reheating temperature](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-do-I-reheat-leftovers-safely). The time will depend on the portion size and depth. Remove the foil for the last **5-10 minutes** if you want to brown the top again, then let it rest briefly before serving.
