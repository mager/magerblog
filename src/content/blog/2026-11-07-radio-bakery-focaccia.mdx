**Draft for a first bake:** The video is the method reference, but its transcript does not give a complete weighed formula. The amounts below are my proposed home test batch, not Radio's published quantities. I have not baked it yet. I also checked [Justine Doiron's home adaptation](https://justinesnacks.com/radio-bakery-sourdough-focaccia/), which supplies measurements for a much wetter dough. Mine starts at 80% hydration and uses a smaller dough load, no added sugar, cooked pork sausage, and a 500°F home oven. Those are changes to test, not claims about how Radio makes its bread.

## Plan for three days

This needs an **established, active sourdough starter**, fed with equal weights of flour and water. Making a new starter is a separate project. A small amount of instant yeast goes into the final dough too.

- **Friday evening:** Mix the levain, a small batch of starter built for this loaf.
- **Saturday:** Mix the dough, fold it, let it rise, and move it into the pan. Refrigerate after the pan rise.
- **Sunday:** Let the dough warm and rise, then top and bake it.

Allow roughly **30–42 hours overall**, including the two nights. Active work is about 50 minutes; the prep time above excludes fermentation and cooling. Reserve about **4–6 hours on the mixing day** and **3–5 hours on baking day**. The rise can take longer in a cool kitchen.

## Ingredients

Use a scale for the dough. Cups and spoons are approximate, especially for flour and bubbly starter.

### Levain: mix the night before

- **10 g active sourdough starter**, about **2 teaspoons**, at 100% hydration
- **40 g bread flour**, about **⅓ cup**, spooned and leveled
- **5 g whole-wheat flour**, about **2 teaspoons**
- **45 g cool water**, about **3 tablespoons**

This makes **100 g levain**; use all of it in the dough.

### Final dough

- **450 g bread flour**, about **3¾ cups**, spooned and leveled
- **350 g water**, about **1 cup plus 7 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons**, divided: **320 g** for mixing and **30 g** for the salt
- **100 g ripe levain**, all of the batch above, roughly **⅓–½ cup** depending on its rise
- **10 g fine sea salt**, about **1⅔ teaspoons**
- **0.4 g instant yeast**, about **⅛ teaspoon**

Including the levain, this is **500 g total flour and 400 g total water: 80% hydration**. The dough weighs about **910 g**. There is no oil mixed into it.

### Pan and toppings

- **1 teaspoon olive oil**, for the rising container
- **2 tablespoons olive oil**, for the pan
- **½ cup crème fraîche**
- **4 ounces (115 g) fully cooked, crumbled pork sausage**, about **¾ cup**, cooled and drained
- **2 tablespoons olive oil**, for the top before baking
- **¼ cup finely sliced fresh chives**
- **1 tablespoon olive oil**, to finish

The sausage amount is its **cooked weight**. Prepare it before starting the topping step. Keep the toppings refrigerated until needed. This test uses ordinary pork sausage in place of Radio's house mixture.

## Instructions

1. **Build the levain.** Mix **10 g active starter (about 2 teaspoons), 40 g bread flour (⅓ cup), 5 g whole-wheat flour (2 teaspoons), and 45 g cool water (3 tablespoons)** in a jar with room to expand. Cover loosely and leave at room temperature for about **8–12 hours**. Use it when it has roughly doubled, contains bubbles throughout, and still has a slightly rounded top. If it peaks sooner, use it sooner; if it has barely moved, wait before mixing the dough.

2. **Rest the flour and water.** In a large bowl, mix **450 g bread flour (3¾ cups)** with **320 g water (about 1⅓ cups plus 1 teaspoon)**. Keep the other **30 g water (2 tablespoons)** aside. Cover for **30 minutes**. This flour-and-water rest is called an autolyse.

3. **Finish mixing.** Work in all **100 g ripe levain (roughly ⅓–½ cup)** and **0.4 g instant yeast (⅛ teaspoon)**. Dissolve **10 g fine sea salt (about 1⅔ teaspoons)** in the reserved **30 g water (2 tablespoons)** and work that in too. Pinch and fold by hand until evenly mixed, about **5–8 minutes**. The dough may separate as you add water; keep mixing until it holds together. It will remain very sticky.

4. **Fold and let it rise.** Coat a straight-sided container with **1 teaspoon olive oil**, add the dough, and mark its starting level. Cover. At **30-minute intervals**, do **four sets of folds**: with wet hands, lift one side over the middle, then repeat around the other three sides. Handle the later sets gently. After the fourth set, leave covered until the dough has expanded by about **50–75%**, shows bubbles, and has rounded edges. Allow roughly **3–4 hours from the end of mixing**, including the folds. Use the rise as the check, not just the time.

5. **Move it into the pan.** Spread **2 tablespoons olive oil** over the bottom and lower sides of the **10 × 14-inch Lloyd pan**. Gently transfer the dough and ease it into a rectangle. Cover and rest for **1–2 hours**, until it spreads more easily and looks puffy. Stretch again only if it relaxes without tearing. It can finish reaching the corners tomorrow. Cover with room above the dough and refrigerate for **12–18 hours**. If it expands quickly, put it in the fridge earlier.

6. **Warm and finish the rise.** Take the covered pan out of the fridge **2–4 hours before baking**. As it softens, gently ease the dough into any empty corners. It is ready when there are bubbles across the surface and the whole dough moves with a soft wobble. If it is tight and flat, wait. During the final hour, heat the oven to **500°F (260°C), conventional heat**, with a rack in the lower third. Allow **45–60 minutes** to preheat. Confirm that your pan is rated for this temperature.

7. **Top and dimple.** Spoon **½ cup crème fraîche** across the dough in small patches. Add **4 ounces (115 g; about ¾ cup) cooked, cooled pork sausage** and drizzle with **2 tablespoons olive oil**. With oiled fingers, press dimples through the dough to the pan in one pass across the surface. Keep some bubbles intact; repeated pressing will remove more air.

8. **Bake and check underneath.** Bake at **500°F (260°C)** for about **20–30 minutes**. Start checking at **18 minutes** because a dark pan can brown quickly. Carefully lift an edge with a suitable spatula. The underside should be deeply golden and firm, and the top should have brown patches. If the top is ready first, cover it loosely with foil and continue for a few minutes. Record the time for this oven; the bakery's deck-oven timing will not transfer directly.

9. **Finish and cool.** Rest in the pan for **2 minutes**, then lift onto a wire rack. Scatter over **¼ cup sliced chives** and drizzle with **1 tablespoon olive oil**. Cool for at least **15–20 minutes** before cutting into **8–12 pieces**. The center needs time to set, and the rack helps keep the bottom crisp.
